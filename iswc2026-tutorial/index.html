Semantic-Aware Partitioning of Property Graphs

A half-day tutorial on the principles and techniques for partitioning property graphs across distributed environments while preserving their semantic consistency, covering property graph constraints, balanced graph partitioning algorithms, and constraint-aware partitioning strategies.

Abstract

Property graphs are widely used to model richly connected, attributed data. As such datasets outgrow a single machine, they must be partitioned across machines for storage, query processing, and reasoning. Classical partitioners optimise structural objectives such as edge cut and load balance. Property graphs, however, also carry semantic constraints, including keys, functional dependencies, and generating dependencies. These constraints govern identity, consistency, and inference, and a purely structural cut can split them across partitions.

This half-day tutorial studies how property graphs can be partitioned without losing such semantics. It is organised in four parts: the property graph model and its constraint languages; the foundations of balanced graph partitioning; constraint-aware partitioning, in which constraints guide placement; and a hands-on demonstration on public datasets that contrasts structural and constraint-aware placement. The tutorial closes with open problems across partitioning, reasoning, and the Semantic Web.

Overview

Graph partitioning splits a graph into balanced parts while minimising the edges between them, which is a prerequisite for distributing a graph across machines for storage, query processing, and reasoning. Structural partitioning is well studied, from classical multilevel schemes such as METIS to parallel multi-constraint methods. These optimise topological objectives such as edge cut and load balance, treating the graph as a purely structural object.

Property graphs, however, can also carry semantic constraints such as key constraints, graph functional dependencies, or more general graph generating dependencies. Such constraints may span multiple vertices and edges, so graph partitioning can separate the data they depend on. Checking or reasoning with it may require data from more than one partition.

The tutorial explores graph partitioning from a Semantic Web perspective, emphasising scalability and semantic constraints. The material is organized in four parts. The first introduces property graphs and their constraint languages. The second presents the foundations of graph partitioning. The third covers constraint-aware partitioning, where constraints guide data placement. The fourth provides a hands-on demonstration and discusses open challenges.

The topic is timely for the Semantic Web community. Property graphs are now backed by recent ISO standards: GQL (ISO/IEC 39075:2024) and SQL/PGQ (ISO/IEC 9075-16:2023). Their constraint and schema layer has also matured. PG-Keys and PG-Schema provide constraint and schema languages for property graphs. Recent work also places PG-Schema, SHACL, and ShEx on a common formal foundation. Partitioning property graphs raises the same questions of data placement and constraint preservation that the Semantic Web community already faces with RDF, SPARQL, SHACL, and ShEx.

Materials

All materials will be released before the conference under open licences, including slides, reading materials, datasets, configurations, and reproduction scripts. The demonstration relies on open-source components and benchmark datasets, so attendees can re-run and extend it.

This is the first edition of the tutorial. It builds on the presenters’ tutorial experience at database and Semantic Web venues, including VLDB 2025, ISWC 2024, and EDBT 2019, and extends it to the largely unexplored area of property graph partitioning.
